### Executive Summary

This project is interested in understanding badminton sports aiming to design a VR simulation game for training with the aid of artificial intelligence technology. To do so, efforts will be made on video capturing, badminton simulation, and VR game design with an emphasis on fidelity modeling and realistic visualization taking into consideration of injury prevention purposes.

### Scope

This badminton AI research will include three major parts: Drone-based video capturing, Badminton simulation, and Badminton VR game design. The scope of the research involves computer vision, simulation, and VR game design.

#### Part 1: Drone-based video capturing

1. Single drone-based path planning for video recording to capture the pose of badminton players.
2. Collaborative dual or multiple drone-based path planning for quality video capturing (occlusion avoidance) during badminton plays.
3. Artificial intelligence techniques to better plan the drone motion path.

#### Part 2: Simulation for badminton

1. Simulation study to understand different parameters of game play such as shuttlecock movement, air flow, lighting, etc.
2. Simulation to help analysis of players' response with regards to potential injury.
3. Intelligence simulation in a virtual badminton using digital twin concept.

#### Part 3: VR game design for badminton

1. VR games will be designed for training purposes.
2. Simulation-enabled VR badminton games with a focus on potential injury.
3. VR gaming will be performed to evaluate the effectiveness of the game players in simulated environments.

### Challenges

1. Badminton is a high-speed sport with player training being a critical and lengthy process particularly when injury is taken into consideration.
2. Video recording using a static camera often makes the subtle of the player's pose unfortunately being not properly captured in detail.
3. It will be meaningful to explore potential collaborations with a Shenzhen-based drone company for drone video recording.
4. The realistic simulation of badminton games for an alternative tool.
5. Ethics approval should be sought before volunteers can be engaged in the experiment.
